SIMPLE NOTES: CORRELATION AND COVARIANCE IN STATISTICS

1. CORRELATION

Definition:
Correlation measures the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables.

Example:
As study hours increase, exam marks may also increase.

Types of Correlation:
- Positive correlation: Both variables move in the same direction. Example: study hours and marks.
- Negative correlation: One variable increases while the other decreases. Example: product price and demand, in many situations.
- Zero correlation: No linear relationship between the variables.

Correlation Coefficient:
Pearson's correlation coefficient is represented by r.

Range:
-1 <= r <= +1

Meaning:
- r = +1: Perfect positive correlation.
- r = 0: No linear correlation.
- r = -1: Perfect negative correlation.

Important:
Correlation does not imply causation. Two variables being related does not prove that one causes the other.


2. COVARIANCE

Definition:
Covariance measures whether two variables tend to move together or in opposite directions.

Interpretation:
- Positive covariance: Both variables tend to increase or decrease together.
- Negative covariance: When one variable increases, the other tends to decrease.
- Zero covariance: No linear co-movement is detected by covariance; nonlinear relationships may still exist.

Population Covariance Formula:
Cov(X, Y) = sum[(Xi - mean(X)) * (Yi - mean(Y))] / N

Where:
- Xi, Yi = individual observations.
- mean(X), mean(Y) = population means.
- N = number of observations.

For a sample, the usual formula divides by n - 1 instead of n.


3. DIFFERENCE BETWEEN CORRELATION AND COVARIANCE

Correlation:
- Measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship.
- Always between -1 and +1.
- Unitless.
- Easier to compare across datasets.
- Standardized measure.

Covariance:
- Measures the direction of co-movement.
- Has no fixed numerical range.
- Depends on the units of both variables.
- Its magnitude depends on the variables' scales.
- Unstandardized measure.


EXAM REMINDER

Both correlation and covariance describe relationships between variables. Correlation is standardized, whereas covariance depends on the variables' scales.
